The part that decides everything

You are in charge of the tool

A bot does what you tell it. That is the whole point, and the whole risk.

Automated trading is legitimate. Trading itself is legitimate. Neither one makes money on its own. A bot removes hesitation, tiredness and second-guessing — it will place the trade at 3am and it will stop the moment your target is hit. What it will never do is decide how much you should be risking. That decision was yours before you pressed Start, and it is the decision that separates the traders who last from the ones who do not.

Two people can run the identical bot on the identical market on the identical day. One sets a stake they can afford and a stop loss they respect. The other raises the stake after a win because it feels easy, and removes the stop loss after a loss because it feels unfair. They will not get the same result, and the bot is not the reason.

Greed and overtrading are the two things that empty accounts, and no software can protect you from either. Set your limits while you are calm, then let the bot keep them for you.

Plain English

The words you need to understand before you start

You should not run a tool you cannot explain. Here is every term that matters.

Stake
The money on one single trade. Not your balance — just that one trade. A sensible stake is small enough that a run of losses is survivable and boring, not frightening.
Take profit
The amount of profit at which the bot stops for you. It is a discipline, not a ceiling. The trader who removes it because "it is going well" is the trader who gives it all back.
Stop loss
The amount of loss at which the bot stops. This is the single most important number you will set. Decide it while you are calm, and treat it as already spent the moment you press Start.
Martingale — read this one twice
Martingale means raising your stake after a loss so a single win recovers what the losing run took. It is used by many automated strategies, including some here, and it is not a trick or a scam — it is a well-known method with a well-known failure mode.
It works, until it does not. Because the stake multiplies rather than adds, it grows faster than people expect. Here is a 3.1× ladder starting from a $1 stake:

Seven losses in a row is not rare enough to ignore. That is why your stop loss matters more than the multiplier does, and why the starting stake should be small relative to your balance — not the largest you can afford.
Drawdown
How far your balance has fallen from its highest point. A strategy can be profitable overall and still put you through a drawdown you cannot stomach. Knowing the number you would panic at — before you meet it — is most of risk management.
Win rate, and why it is not the whole story
A bot winning 9 trades out of 10 sounds excellent. If the tenth loses more than the nine won, it is a losing strategy. What matters is the size of the wins against the size of the losses — not how often you are right.
Overtrading
Running longer, or bigger, than you planned — usually to win back a loss, sometimes because a good run feels like a streak. It is the most common way an account that was up ends the week down.
Demo account
A Deriv account funded with practice money. Every bot here runs on it exactly as it runs on a real one. It costs nothing and it is the honest way to find out how a strategy behaves before your own money is involved.

Before you press start

Seven rules worth more than any bot

  1. Decide what you can lose, before you start

    Pick a number that would not change your month if it were gone. That is your stop loss. Everything else is negotiable; this is not.

  2. Start on demo

    Run the bot on practice money until you have seen it lose as well as win. If you have only seen it win, you have not seen it yet.

  3. Keep the stake small relative to your balance

    The stake is not "what I can afford in one go". It is what survives a losing run without forcing you to stop at the worst moment.

  4. Never raise the stake to win a loss back

    This is the instinct that costs the most. The loss is already gone. Raising the stake does not undo it — it only decides how much the next one costs.

  5. Stop when your target is reached

    A take profit you ignore is not a take profit. The bot will stop; the temptation to restart it is yours to manage.

  6. One decision at a time, made in advance

    Set stake, take profit and stop loss while nothing is running. Decisions made mid-run, with money moving, are the ones you regret.

  7. Never trade borrowed money, or money you need

    Rent, fees, someone else's money. If losing it would hurt beyond the number, it does not belong in a trading account at all.
